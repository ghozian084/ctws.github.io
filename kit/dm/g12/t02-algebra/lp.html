Lesson Plan: Algebra (Pure Math 2 & 3)

School: SMA Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 12

Time Allocation: 9 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 1. Algebra

Reference: Cambridge Mathematics Pure Math 2-3 A Level textbook

Method: 5E, Problem Based Learning, Math Modelling Task

Approach: Hands-on Activity

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • 2.1.1: Understand the meaning of $|x|$, sketch the graph of $y = |ax + b|$ and use relations such as $|a − b| = |b − a|$ and $|x − a| < b \iff a − b < x < a + b$ when solving equations and inequalities.
  • 2.1.2 & 3.1.2: Divide a polynomial, of degree not exceeding 4, by a linear or quadratic polynomial, and identify the quotient and remainder.
  • 2.1.3 & 3.1.3: Use the factor theorem to factorise a polynomial of degree not exceeding 4.
  • 2.1.4 & 3.1.4: Use the remainder theorem to find the remainder when a polynomial is divided by a linear polynomial.

📅 Meeting 1: The Modulus Function Concept (Subtopic 1.1)

E1 ENGAGE | Contextual Problem [15 minutes]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher-led)

🪝 Teacher Hook — Slide 1 (10 min):

  • Display a picture of a CNC machining part from a factory in Semarang. "A steel rod must be exactly 50 cm long. The machine has an error tolerance of 0.2 cm."
  • Ask: "What is the acceptable range of lengths?" (49.8 to 50.2). "How do we write the error distance mathematically without caring if it's too long or too short?"

Introduce Notation (5 min):

  • Introduce $|x|$ as the "absolute distance from zero."
E2 EXPLORE | Hands-On Number Line [20 minutes]
Motoric Activity
(Student-led)

Walking the Number Line (20 min):

  • Use masking tape to create a giant number line on the classroom floor.
  • Ask a student to stand at 5. Ask another to stand at 2. "Walk to each other. How many steps?" (3).
  • Now swap: Stand at 2, walk to 5. "How many steps?" (3).
  • Critical Thinking: Students deduce that physical distance is always positive, proving $|a - b| = |b - a|$.
E3 EXPLAIN | Solving Simple Modulus Equations [20 minutes]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher-led)

Direct Instruction — Slides 2–4 (20 min):

  • Slide 2: Formal definition: $|x| = x$ if $x \ge 0$, and $|x| = -x$ if $x < 0$.
  • Slide 3: Solving $|2x - 3| = 7$. Model splitting into two cases: $2x - 3 = 7$ OR $2x - 3 = -7$.
  • Slide 4: Solving equations with two moduli: $|3x + 1| = |x - 5|$. (Square both sides to remove modulus).
Scaffold students: Low achievers should always write "Positive Case" and "Negative Case" as explicit headers before doing the algebra to prevent forgetting the second answer.
E4 & E5 | Application & Evaluation [25 minutes]
Independent Practice
& Exit Slip

Elaborate (15 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 1 solving linear modulus equations.

🎟️ Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Solve for $x$: $|x - 4| = 2x + 1$. Check for extraneous solutions!

📅 Meeting 2: Graphing $y = |f(x)|$ (Subtopic 1.2)

E1 ENGAGE | Laser Reflection Hook [10 minutes]
Visual Q&A
(Teacher-led)

🪝 Teacher Hook — Slide 1 (10 min):

  • "If I fire a laser beam at the x-axis, it bounces off perfectly like a mirror."
  • Draw the line $y = 2x - 4$ dipping below the x-axis. "If the x-axis is a solid floor, what happens to the negative y-values?" (They bounce up!).
E2 EXPLORE | Tracing Paper Folding [20 minutes]
Hands-On Activity
(Student-led)

Paper Folding Reflection (20 min):

  • Provide students with tracing paper and axes pre-drawn.
  • Students draw $y = x - 3$. Then, they fold the paper EXACTLY along the x-axis.
  • They trace the negative part of the line onto the positive side, creating the classic 'V' shape of $y = |x - 3|$.
  • Creativity: Students visually understand that modulus applies a mathematical "reflection" to negative outputs.
E3 EXPLAIN | Vertex and Intercepts [20 minutes]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher-led)

Direct Instruction — Slides 2–4 (20 min):

  • Slide 2: Steps to graph $y = |ax + b|$: Find the x-intercept (the vertex where the bounce happens) and the y-intercept.
  • Slide 3: Solving equations graphically. Draw $y = |x - 2|$ and $y = 3$. Find where they intersect.
  • Slide 4: Why extraneous solutions happen (the algebra finds the 'ghost' line that was reflected away).
Scaffold students: EAL students annotate the graph with terms "Vertex", "y-intercept", and "Reflected line".
E4 & E5 | Application & Evaluation [30 minutes]
Independent Task
& Exit Slip

Elaborate (20 min):

  • Worksheet 2. Students sketch various modulus graphs and find intersection points with horizontal lines.

🎟️ Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Find the coordinates of the vertex and the y-intercept for the graph of $y = |3x + 6|$.

📅 Meeting 3: Solving Modulus Inequalities (Subtopic 1.3)

E1 & E2 | Modelling Tolerances [20 minutes]
PBL Exploration
(Group Work)

🪝 Math Modeling Task (20 min):

  • "A server room must be kept at $20^\circ\text{C}$. An alarm sounds if the temperature $T$ fluctuates by more than $3^\circ\text{C}$."
  • Ask groups to write this as a mathematical inequality WITHOUT modulus ($17 < T < 23$) and WITH modulus ($|T - 20| < 3$).
  • Collaboration: Groups map the logic: $|x| < a$ means "trapped inside", while $|x| > a$ means "escaped outside".
E3 EXPLAIN | Inequality Logic [25 minutes]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher-led)

Direct Instruction — Slides 1–4 (25 min):

  • Slide 1: Formalize the theorem: $|x - a| < b \iff a - b < x < a + b$.
  • Slide 2: Model solving $|2x - 1| \ge 5$. Split into $2x - 1 \ge 5$ OR $2x - 1 \le -5$.
  • Slide 3: Graphical interpretation. Why does "$<$" create a connected line segment, and "$>$" create two separate outward arrows?
  • Slide 4: Squaring both sides for $|ax + b| < |cx + d|$.
Scaffold students: Teach the mnemonic "Great-OR" (Greater than splits into OR) and "Less-THAND" (Less than merges into AND).
E4 & E5 | Application & Evaluation [35 minutes]
Independent Task

Elaborate (25 min):

  • Worksheet 3. Solve inequalities algebraically and shade solutions on number lines.

🎟️ Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Solve the inequality $|x + 2| < 3x - 4$.

📅 Meeting 4: Division of Polynomials (Linear Divisors) (Subtopic 1.4)

E1 & E2 | The Area Model Reborn [25 minutes]
Hands-On Modeling

🪝 Hook (5 min):

  • "If we know the volume of a box is $x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x - 12$ and the height is $x-2$, how do we find the base area? We must divide polynomials!"

Explore — The Grid Method (20 min):

  • Provide whiteboards. Show how to do division using an Area/Grid model (reverse of expansion).
  • Students deduce what must go in the top header to make the inside boxes equal the dividend.
  • Self-Regulated: Students realize this is a puzzle of finding missing pieces.
E3 EXPLAIN | Algebraic Long Division [25 minutes]
Direct Instruction

Direct Instruction — Slides 1–4 (25 min):

  • Slide 1: Compare polynomial long division directly to primary school numerical long division.
  • Slide 2: Model dividing $2x^3 - 5x^2 + x - 7$ by $(x - 3)$.
  • Slide 3: The golden rule: Write the answer as $P(x) = D(x)Q(x) + R(x)$.
  • Slide 4: What happens when a term is missing? (e.g., $x^3 - 8$). Must insert placeholders ($+ 0x^2 + 0x$)!
Scaffold students: Allow students who struggle with long division syntax to use the Grid/Tabular method or Synthetic Division for linear divisors.
E4 & E5 | Application & Evaluation [30 minutes]
Independent Task

Elaborate (20 min):

  • Worksheet 4. Perform division and state the quotient and remainder.

🎟️ Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Divide $x^3 - 2x^2 + 5$ by $x + 1$. State the remainder.

📅 Meeting 5: Division by Quadratic Polynomials (Subtopic 1.4)

E1 & E2 | Scaling Up Complexity [20 minutes]
Teacher-led &
Group Work

🪝 Hook & Explore (20 min):

  • "What if the base area of our box is given as a quadratic, like $x^2 + 2x - 1$? Can we still divide a degree-4 polynomial by it?"
  • Groups attempt to set up the long division. The structure is the same, but the tracking requires extreme neatness.
E3 EXPLAIN | Managing Quadratic Divisors [20 minutes]
Direct Instruction

Direct Instruction — Slides 1-3 (20 min):

  • Slide 1: Model dividing $x^4 + 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 5$ by $x^2 - x + 2$.
  • Slide 2: Highlight that the remainder can now be linear ($ax + b$), not just a constant.
  • Slide 3: Equating coefficients method as a powerful alternative to long division. Let $P(x) = (x^2+px+q)(x^2+cx+d) + (rx+s)$ and expand.
Scaffold students: Extension students must use the Equating Coefficients method to solve for unknowns in a division identity.
E4 & E5 | Mastery Practice [40 minutes]
Independent Task

Elaborate (30 min) & Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Worksheet 5. High-concentration long division tasks.
  • Exit Ticket: What is the maximum possible degree of the remainder when dividing by a quadratic polynomial? (Answer: Degree 1, linear).

📅 Meeting 6: The Factor Theorem (Subtopic 1.5)

E1 & E2 | Discovery via Substitution [20 minutes]
PBL Discovery

🪝 Hook & Explore (20 min):

  • "Long division takes a long time. What if we just want to know IF something is a perfect factor (remainder zero) without doing the division?"
  • Have half the class divide $x^3 - x^2 - 4x + 4$ by $(x - 2)$.
  • Have the other half calculate the value of $P(2)$.
  • Compare results. They are both Zero!
E3 EXPLAIN | Formalizing the Theorem [20 minutes]
Direct Instruction

Direct Instruction — Slides 1-3 (20 min):

  • Slide 1: The Factor Theorem: If $P(c) = 0$, then $(x - c)$ is a factor. Conversely, if $(x - c)$ is a factor, $P(c) = 0$.
  • Slide 2: General form: If $P(b/a) = 0$, then $(ax - b)$ is a factor.
  • Slide 3: Using the theorem to fully factorise a cubic equation. (Guess a root, use theorem, then divide to find the remaining quadratic).
E4 & E5 | Application & Evaluation [40 minutes]
Independent Task

Elaborate (30 min) & Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Worksheet 6. Find roots and fully factorise cubic and quartic equations.
  • Exit Ticket: Given $(x + 3)$ is a factor of $x^3 + kx^2 - x - 6$, find the value of $k$.

📅 Meeting 7: The Remainder Theorem (Subtopic 1.6)

E1 & E2 | Extending the Logic [20 minutes]
Teacher-led &
Pair Work

🪝 Hook & Explore (20 min):

  • "If $P(c) = 0$ means no remainder, what does it mean if $P(c) = 5$?"
  • Pairs test this: Calculate $P(1)$ for $P(x) = x^2 + 2x + 4$. Then divide it by $(x - 1)$. Compare the remainder to $P(1)$.
  • Critical Thinking: Students generalize that $P(c)$ ALWAYS equals the remainder!
E3 EXPLAIN | Simultaneous Equations Context [20 minutes]
Direct Instruction

Direct Instruction — Slides 1-3 (20 min):

  • Slide 1: The Remainder Theorem: When $P(x)$ is divided by $(ax - b)$, the remainder is $P(b/a)$.
  • Slide 2: Prove it algebraically: $P(x) = (ax - b)Q(x) + R$. If we sub $x = b/a$, the $Q(x)$ term becomes zero, leaving $P(b/a) = R$.
  • Slide 3: Solving for unknown coefficients. "When divided by $(x-1)$, remainder is 4. When divided by $(x+2)$, remainder is 16." Set up and solve simultaneous equations.
Scaffold students: SEN/Low students use a color-coding system to keep track of Equation 1 and Equation 2 before solving simultaneously.
E4 & E5 | Advanced Problem Solving [40 minutes]
Independent Task

Elaborate (30 min) & Exit Ticket (10 min):

  • Worksheet 7. Finding unknown constants using the Remainder Theorem.
  • Exit Ticket: Find the remainder when $2x^3 - x^2 + 4x - 1$ is divided by $(2x - 1)$.

📅 Meeting 8: Mathematical Modelling & Complex Synthesis

E1 & E2 | The Engineering Task [20 minutes]
PBL Group Work

🪝 Math Modeling Task (20 min):

  • Culturally STEM: "A structural engineer is designing a curved bridge support in Semarang. The stress curve is modeled by $P(x) = x^4 + ax^3 + bx^2 - 16x - 12$. The support must anchor perfectly (zero stress) at $x = -2$ and $x = 3$."
  • Formulate: Groups must translate "anchor perfectly" into "is a factor of" and set up the math model.
E3 & E4 | Execute & Validate [40 minutes]
Collaborative Solving

Solve & Interpret (40 min):

  • Groups use the Factor Theorem to find $a$ and $b$.
  • Then, they must fully factorise the quartic polynomial to find the other two anchor points on the ground.
  • Collaboration: Groups present their solutions on the whiteboard, defending their mathematical steps.
E5 | Formative Review [20 minutes]
Peer Assessment

Closure (20 min):

  • Review common errors seen during the PBL task (e.g., sign errors in simultaneous equations). Prepare for the exam.

Summative Test

🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Introduce rational root theorem for finding the first factor of a cubic. Challenge them with modulus inequalities involving rational functions, e.g., $\left| \frac{2x-1}{x+3} \right| \ge 4$.
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Mandate the "squaring both sides" method for modulus inequalities rather than logical branching, as it provides a strict algebraic routine. Provide the Grid/Tabular method template for polynomial division.
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a glossary: Quotient (Hasil bagi), Remainder (Sisa), Divisor (Pembagi), Absolute Value (Nilai mutlak). Ensure they understand "exactly divisible by" means remainder equals zero.
SEN (Special Educational Needs) Allow use of highlighters to map corresponding terms during long division subtraction steps to prevent sign errors. Break multi-part word problems into bullet points.

📝 Worksheets Compilation (For Practice & Homework)

Worksheet 1 & 2: Modulus Functions & Graphs

1. Solve the equation: $|3x - 2| = 7$.

2. Solve the equation: $|2x + 5| = |x - 4|$.

3. Sketch the graph of $y = |2x - 6|$ for $-1 \le x \le 5$. State the coordinates of the vertex and the y-intercept.

4. On the same axes, sketch $y = |x - 2|$ and $y = \frac{1}{2}x + 1$. Hence, find the coordinates of their points of intersection.

Worksheet 3: Modulus Inequalities

1. Solve the inequality $|x - 5| < 3$ and represent the solution on a number line.

2. Solve the inequality $|2x + 1| \ge 7$.

3. Find the set of values of $x$ satisfying $|3x - 1| < |x + 5|$.

Worksheet 4 & 5: Polynomial Division

1. Find the quotient and remainder when $x^3 - 4x^2 + 2x + 5$ is divided by $(x - 2)$.

2. Find the quotient and remainder when $2x^4 - 3x^3 + x - 7$ is divided by $x^2 - x + 2$.

Worksheet 6 & 7: Factor and Remainder Theorems

1. Show that $(x + 3)$ is a factor of $P(x) = 2x^3 + x^2 - 13x + 6$. Hence, fully factorise $P(x)$.

2. The polynomial $x^3 + ax^2 + bx - 4$ is exactly divisible by $(x-1)$ and leaves a remainder of $16$ when divided by $(x+3)$. Find $a$ and $b$.

💯 Chapter 1 Evaluation (Used in Meeting 9)

Instructions: Answer all questions. Show all working steps clearly. (Cambridge A-Level Style)

Part A: Modulus Functions (15 Marks)

1. Solve the inequality $|3x - 2| > |x + 4|$. (5 marks)

2. Sketch the graph of $y = |2x + 3|$. (3 marks)

3. Solve the equation $|5x - 1| = 3x + 7$. You must clearly show how you reject any extraneous roots. (7 marks)

Part B: Polynomial Division (10 Marks)

4. The polynomial $P(x) = x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x + 5$ is divided by $(x^2 - x + 1)$.
Find the quotient and the remainder. (6 marks)

5. Find the remainder when $4x^3 - 5x + 2$ is divided by $(2x - 1)$. (4 marks)

Part C: Theorems & Modeling Synthesis (15 Marks)

6. A polynomial is defined by $f(x) = 2x^3 + px^2 + qx - 15$, where $p$ and $q$ are constants.
a) Given that $(x - 3)$ is a factor of $f(x)$, write down an equation in $p$ and $q$. (2 marks)
b) When $f(x)$ is divided by $(x + 2)$, the remainder is $-35$. Write down a second equation in $p$ and $q$. (2 marks)
c) Solve these equations simultaneously to find $p$ and $q$. (4 marks)
d) With these values of $p$ and $q$, completely factorise $f(x)$. (7 marks)
